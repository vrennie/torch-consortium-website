The Institute of Tropical Medicine Antwerp (ITM) is one of the world's leading institutes for training and research in tropical medicine and the organisation of health care in developing countries. Therefore, the TORCH is excited to be able to work closely together with the ITM mycobacteriology research group of Prof. Bouke De Jong. [The Unit of Mycobacteriology](https://www.itg.be/N/dienst-mycobacteriologie#/) is dedicated to improving the health of patients suffering from mycobacterial diseases such as tuberculosis, leprosy, and Buruli ulcer.

[**Prof. Dr. Bouke De Jong**](https://pure.itg.be/nl/persons/bouke-de-jong(76611b1a-0fcc-4acc-98c6-a3607ef4fa89).html)
Since 2010 Dr. de Jong heads the Unit of Mycobacteriology at the Institute of Tropical Medicine. Besides tuberculosis, she studies Buruli Ulcer and leprosy, focusing on improved diagnostics and treatment, as well as molecular epidemiological studies on the impact of public health interventions to reduce transmission, for which she was supported by an ERC starting grant. Together with a very capable team she (co)supervises PhD students from different (endemic) countries.

[**Prof. Dr. Leen Rigouts**](https://pure.itg.be/nl/persons/leen-rigouts(dd9b89e3-c6a4-4883-b3a2-d0e01bd2ac79).html)
Prof. Rigouts is a professor in tropical infectious diseases at the University of Antwerp (UA), and from the start at ITM she was mentor of many bachelor and master students. In 2018 the Torch consortium together with Prof. Rigouts was awarded a [TBM grant](/tbm) for conducting a Trial to compare Whole Genome Sequencing with the standard of care for diagnosis and treatment of Tuberculosis.

[**Dr. Conor Meehan**](https://pure.itg.be/nl/persons/conor-meehan(3aa6e72e-709d-4b58-8e3a-40e9680af43a).html)
Dr. Meehan’s career has primarily involved the use of computational techniques to investigate the evolution and epidemiology of microbes. His areas of expertise include: comparative genomics; whole genome sequencing and assembly; Maximum likelihood and Bayesian phylogenetics; evolution of drug resistance in viral and bacterial pathogens; microbial community ecology and microbiomes; lateral gene transfer; phylodynamics; pathogen transmission and population dynamics. Together with Conor, the TORCH consortium organizes various [VLIR courses](https://torch-consortium.com/vliruos/) for capacity building of TB research in low income countries, including Peru and Ethiopia.

<div class="collaboration-cryptic-logo"><img src="/assets/media/e623f5fd-unnamed.png" alt="CRyPTIC consortium logo" loading="lazy"></div>

The TORCH consortium is an active member of the Comprehensive Resistance Prediction for Tuberculosis, an International Consortium ([CRyPTIC](http://www.crypticproject.org)).The CRyPTIC consortium is led by Prof. Derrick Crook of Oxford University, UK, and funded by the MRC Newton Fund, Wellcome Trust, and the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation. CRyPTIC is a world-wide collaboration between TB research institutions all over the world.

The ultimate goal is to achieve sufficiently accurate genetic prediction of resistance to most anti-tuberculosis drugs, so that WGS can replace culture-based DST for TB. This will enable rapid-turnaround near-to-patient assays to revolutionise MDR-TB identification and management. The Odysseus TORCH team has contributed by sequencing isolates and submitting the data to the worldwide survey of genetic variation associated with resistance to 13 antitubercular drugs. The matched genotypic/phenotypic data collected by the CRyPTIC consortium is an invaluable resource to accelerate diagnostic development and for identification of remaining knowledge gaps.

    <h2 id="caprisa-heading">CAPRISA</h2>
    <p class="collaboration-full-name">Centre for the AIDS Programme of Research in South Africa</p>
  </div>

  <p><a href="https://www.caprisa.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CAPRISA</a>, based in Durban, South Africa, is a research organisation with expertise across infectious diseases, including tuberculosis. CAPRISA participates in the DiSrupT RR-TB study, which investigates how whole-genome sequencing directly from sputum can support improved RR-TB care and transmission control in KwaZulu-Natal.</p>

  <p>Under the study's preliminary sample-flow arrangements, CAPRISA staff are responsible for arranging sample collection and transport from designated NHLS collection points to CAPRISA and for performing real-time sequencing during the intervention period. CAPRISA's involvement contributes locally based sequencing capacity to the study.</p>
</section>

    <h2 id="aurum-heading">The Aurum Institute</h2>
  </div>

  <p><a href="https://www.auruminstitute.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Aurum Institute</a> is a South African health research and implementation organisation working to translate evidence into practice, with a focus that includes tuberculosis, HIV, diagnostics, and health systems.</p>

  <p>In the DiSrupT RR-TB study, Aurum has a key operational role during the intervention period. Under preliminary sample-flow arrangements, Aurum is responsible for collecting study-specific samples from eligible participants at RR-TB treatment initiation sites in the intervention area and ensuring appropriate sample identification, documentation, and handover for transport through routine NHLS services.</p>
</section>

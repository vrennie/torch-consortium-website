## Direct on sputum sequencing to interrupt transmission of rifampicin-resistant tuberculosis

DiSrupT RR-TB is a five-year research project (2026–2030) led by Prof. Annelies Van Rie at the University of Antwerp and funded by the European Research Council (ERC). The project investigates how genomic technologies can improve the treatment and prevention of rifampicin-resistant tuberculosis (RR-TB) and help curb the emergence and spread of resistance to bedaquiline, a cornerstone of modern drug-resistant TB treatment.

## About the study

DiSrupT RR-TB is a pragmatic cluster-randomised trial in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. The study compares a genomics-guided intervention with standard care across four health districts, evaluating whether the integration of genomic information into routine healthcare can improve patient outcomes and reduce transmission in a high-burden setting.

The intervention combines whole-genome sequencing (WGS), prioritising sequencing directly from sputum using the QIAseq xHYB Mtb assay and Illumina technology, with culture-based sequencing where needed. The [MICK-MAGMA platform](/mick-magma/) automates the analysis and interpretation of genomic data, providing healthcare professionals with drug resistance profiles and treatment decision support. Genomic epidemiology and spatial analysis are used to investigate transmission patterns and inform targeted public health responses. The study also evaluates near-point-of-care molecular screening of close contacts to support earlier diagnosis and prevention.

## Research objectives

The trial evaluates four key outcomes:

- Reduction in recent transmission of rifampicin-resistant TB.
- Prevention of an increase in the proportion of bedaquiline-resistant TB attributable to transmission rather than acquired resistance.
- More timely initiation of effective treatment for people with bedaquiline-resistant TB.
- Improved treatment outcomes for people with bedaquiline-resistant TB.

By evaluating these approaches in routine healthcare, DiSrupT RR-TB aims to generate evidence for integrating genomics into TB control programmes in high-burden, resource-constrained settings and to inform future diagnostic, treatment and prevention policies.
